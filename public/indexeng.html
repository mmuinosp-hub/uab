OIKOS

Don't play with food.

A game of negotiation, production and economic decisions in which players exchange resources and choose how to produce over successive sessions.

OIKOS includes three economic games: Capital, Anarquía y Plan.

In the games, you can act as an administrator and/or as a player:

  • Players negotiate and make contracts with other players, make deliveries and produce.
  • The administrator starts the game, adds players, manages the phases of each session and validates contracts.

📚 How it works

In all three games, each player starts with quantities of wheat and iron.

The game develops through successive sessions, with two phases in each:

  • Phase 1: Deliveries: The player negotiates and makes contracts with other players. Each player can make up to five deliveries of the materials they have to other players.
  • Phase 2: Production: Each player chooses a single production process, which may be different in each session:
    • Process 1: Uses 280 wheat and 12 iron to produce 575 wheat (or an equivalent proportion).
    • Process 2: Uses 120 wheat and 8 iron to produce 20 iron (or an equivalent proportion).
    • Process 3: Available materials are reduced by half.

Production consumes all the materials, and in the next session only the resulting production is available.

The delivery and production cycle repeats every session.

Game 1: Capital

We simulate capitalism in its most essential form: agents who exchange and produce to maximize their profit.

The winner is the player with the greatest wheat·a + iron·b in the last session, where a and b are set by the administrator and are not disclosed until the game ends. The last session is not announced either.

Players may sign contracts to guarantee deliveries and form companies.

Game 2: Anarchy

We simulate a market with different objectives: agents who exchange and produce to maximize their individual objectives.

Objectives can be set as follows:

  • The same for all players: for example, the winner is the player with the highest total quantity of wheat consumed across all sessions.
  • Different for each player: a player is then compared with themselves, rather than with the others, across other games or previous sessions.

Players may sign contracts to guarantee deliveries and form cooperatives.

Game 3: Plan

We simulate extreme planning: the planner allocates without a market to achieve a given objective.

Deliveries take place between production processes under each planner’s control, with no negotiation or deliveries with other planners. Each planner needs a separate room with three virtual players so that all production processes can be used.

The objective can be defined as in Capital or Anarchy.

Variants

The games can be compared, for example Plan with Capital, so that some players play Capital while the others try to beat their results with Plan.

They can also be combined, so that some players play Anarchy while the others play Capital, with everyone exchanging in the same market.

We recommend playing Capital first with the default variants. However, the administrator can:

  • Define different initial resources for each player (by default, everyone starts with 1000 wheat and 1000 iron).
  • Modify the information available to players (by default, all deliveries and quantities are known, but not the last session, nor a and b in Capital).
  • Set the maximum number of deliveries (by default, 5 or fewer).
  • Modify contract conditions, including compensation for lost profits (by default, whoever breaches loses);
  • Modify company conditions (by default, with 4 or fewer members).
  • Set a redistribution of a percentage of wheat and iron shared among all players (by default, no redistribution).

Contracts

In Capital and Anarchy, contracts can be signed with up to 4 players.

To be effective, contracts must be delivered to the administrator using the means they establish for validation (identified emails, for example).

Delivery contracts:

Contracts specify the participants’ names, the quantities delivered by each one, and the sessions in which deliveries will take place; future-session deliveries may be agreed.

If a contract specifies deliveries in a session later than the one that turns out to be the last, later deliveries are ignored.

Without a contract, it is allowed not to reciprocate a delivery.

Company and cooperative contracts:

A company or cooperative may be formed through a contract specifying the names of the participants (up to 4) and the company or cooperative name.

Participants continue playing as before, making deliveries and contracts individually, but:

  • When measuring objective performance, the wheat and iron held by all participants in each session are considered to be divided equally among them. For example, in Capital, the wheat and iron of all participants in the last session are considered divided equally among them.
  • Participants are jointly liable for delivery contracts signed by any member.

A player may belong to only one company or cooperative.

A company or cooperative may not be dissolved except by unanimous agreement of the participants or as otherwise provided by the founding contract.

OIKOS is an experiment for testing economic theory and also a teaching tool. It can be used freely, without registration or participation in the research; however, we would greatly appreciate permission to analyze the results and would acknowledge this in resulting publications if desired. The game was inspired by Alfons Barceló, «Juegos de simulación con modelos económicos reproductivos», El Trimestre Económico, Vol. 56, Nº 223(3) (1989), and the first results of the research were presented at the X Jornadas de Análisis Input-Output (2024) and at the 32nd International Input-Output Association Conference (2026).
